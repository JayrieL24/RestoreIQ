# Service page — signature band photography

Ten photographs, one per service page. They appear in the same section on every
page, directly under the section heading, so they must read as one set shot by
one person on one job.

All ten are being regenerated together. The three that exist today
(`drying-progress`, `smoke-residue`, `containment`) are the reference for the
look but are **not** being kept — regenerate them too, to the same filenames.

---

## The look

These are not marketing photographs. They are the photographs a restoration
technician takes on their phone to attach to a job file: evidence of a
condition, taken quickly, in bad light, in a building that is mid-job.

Everything below applies to all ten. Only the subject changes.

**Camera.** A recent phone's main camera, handheld. Roughly 26mm equivalent, so
there is mild wide-angle stretch at the edges and strong depth of field. Not a
DSLR, not a prime lens, not a tripod. Slightly off-level is correct and
desirable.

**Light.** Available light only. Overcast daylight through a window out of
frame, or a plain ceiling fixture, or a single work light. Slightly
underexposed, with the shadows going flat and a little noisy. White balance
cool and slightly green indoors. Phone HDR has lifted the shadows a touch
unevenly — that unevenness should be visible.

**Colour.** Muted and desaturated throughout: grey, beige, dirty white, dull
brown. The only saturated colour in any frame is equipment — safety yellow,
blue nitrile gloves, blue painter's tape, an orange vacuum. Nothing else is
bright.

**Materials are used and dirty.** Scuffed baseboard, stained carpet, chipped
paint, dusty subfloor, scratched tool bodies, worn cabinet edges. The building
is lived in and the job is half done. Dirt and wear are the subject, not a
blemish on it.

**Depth.** The thing being examined is sharp. The room behind falls soft, but
only moderately — a phone at f/1.8 on a close subject, not a portrait-mode
cutout. No bokeh balls.

### Framing — important

The photo sits in a wrapper that crops to fill a flexible height, so the edges
of the frame are unreliable. **Keep the subject in the middle 60% of the
frame.** Nothing that matters — the tool, the hand, the damage, the detail the
caption refers to — should sit near an edge or in a corner.

### Composition varies, deliberately

Do not shoot all ten the same distance away. The set should feel like one
person working through a job, not ten versions of one photograph. Use the
distance each brief specifies:

- **Close** — hand and tool fill much of the frame, room barely visible
- **Working distance** — a person's natural view of what they are doing
- **Room** — standing back, no hands, the whole condition visible

Where a hand appears it is bare or in a blue nitrile glove, with a dark
long-sleeve work layer. It is doing the task, not demonstrating it. Never a
face, never a full body, never eye contact, never a pose.

### Do not produce

- Clean, new, polished, staged or showroom-looking interiors
- Sunshine, warm golden light, lens flare, god rays, bright airy rooms
- Glossy highlights, HDR glow, heavy vignette, creamy background blur
- A smiling technician, a visible face, a branded or pristine uniform
- Symmetrical hero composition, drone views, ceiling-corner angles
- Readable text, logos, or brand marks on tools, packaging or clothing
- Over-sharpened micro-contrast, plastic skin, that rendered CGI surface

If it looks like stock photography or a 3D render, it is wrong. If it looks
slightly boring and a bit badly shot, it is right.

## The ten

### 1. `drying-progress.jpg` — Water damage · **close**

Camera near floor level. A bare hand enters from the lower left holding a
pinless moisture meter flat against a white baseboard. The carpet in front is
soaked dark in an irregular tide line, with grit and debris in the wet pile.
The baseboard above is swollen and its paint is lifting. A yellow air mover
sits soft in the background, cable trailing across the carpet.

> Nearly a floor-level view. The meter and the wet carpet edge are the sharpest
> things in frame. The room beyond is soft and unremarkable.

### 2. `smoke-residue.jpg` — Fire and smoke · **working distance**

A kitchen wall cabinet coated in brown-grey smoke film, heaviest at the top and
fading downward. A blue-gloved hand at the right works a white chemical sponge
down the wall beside it, leaving one clean stripe through the residue. The
contrast between the cleaned stripe and the film around it is the subject.

> Standing, eye level, angled along the cabinet run. Stove and utensils soft in
> the lower left. The clean stripe sits centre-frame.

### 3. `containment.jpg` — Sewage · **room**

A doorway sealed floor to ceiling with clear plastic sheeting, taped to the
frame with blue painter's tape, a zipper run down the middle. A work light
behind it glows through the plastic. A wet vacuum and a coiled black hose sit
in the foreground. No hands in this frame.

> Standing in a hallway, shooting straight at the sealed doorway. Domestic and
> ordinary around the edges — a banister, a towel, a roll of tape on the floor.

### 4. `pipe-cavity.jpg` — Burst pipe · **close**

A rectangular inspection hole cut into drywall at knee height, exposing a stud
bay with a copper supply line running through it. The cut edges are ragged and
there is drywall dust on the floor below. The timber inside is visibly darker
where it is wet. A bare hand at lower left holds a moisture meter against the
stud inside the opening.

> Camera low and close to the wall. Drywall dust on dark flooring is the main
> foreground texture. The dark wet timber inside the bay is the focal point.

### 5. `appliance-cavity.jpg` — Appliance leak · **working distance**

A dishwasher pulled forward out of a kitchen cabinet run, standing on the
floor at frame left with its hoses visible behind. The exposed subfloor where
it stood is stained dark in an oval. A bare hand holds a moisture meter flat
against that stain. The removed kick-board leans against the cabinet.

> Crouched, looking into the gap. The cabinet carcass edge is swollen and
> delaminating slightly at its base. Dull indoor light, no window visible.

### 6. `hardwood-mats.jpg` — Hardwood drying · **working distance**

Flat floor-drying mats taped down across visibly cupped oak boards, a clear
hose running from them out of frame. Camera very low so the cupping reads
against the raking light as a ripple across the floor.

> Tape lifting slightly at one corner. Board seams darker than the faces. A
> yellow air mover soft in the background. The ripple in the floor is the
> subject and belongs centre-frame.

### 7. `crawlspace-barrier.jpg` — Crawlspace · **close**

Under a floor, looking along bare soil beneath timber joists. A black polythene
vapour barrier is torn open across the middle, damp soil showing through the
tear. A work light at the back throws a hard pool of light down the space.

> Cramped and low — the frame should feel enclosed. Joists and subfloor
> directly overhead, piers receding into dark. Dust visible in the light beam.
> Darker than the rest of the set; that is correct here.

### 8. `commercial-zone.jpg` — Commercial · **room**

A floor-to-ceiling plastic containment wall with a zipped door, dividing a
closed work area from an open commercial floor. Through the slightly cloudy
plastic, yellow equipment is visible as soft shapes. The near side of the floor
is clean and still in use. No hands in this frame.

> Standing on the open side. Plain suspended ceiling tiles and strip lighting
> overhead. Absolutely no signage, branding or readable text anywhere.

### 9. `contents-inventory.jpg` — Contents · **working distance**

A folding packing table with several paper-wrapped household items on it.
Blue-gloved hands at lower right photograph one labelled item using a phone. A
clipboard with a part-filled inventory sheet sits beside it.

> The label is a plain sticker with handwriting suggested but not legible. Brown
> paper, bubble wrap, one open crate. Garage or warehouse, flat overhead light.

### 10. `rebuild-finish.jpg` — Reconstruction · **working distance**

A taped and sanded drywall seam running up a wall, joint compound feathered and
dry, sanding dust across the floor below. A length of new primed baseboard
rests against the wall waiting to be fitted. A bare hand at lower left holds a
sanding block against the seam.

> The new baseboard is obviously cleaner and newer than the wall beside it —
> that contrast is the subject. Wall bare and unpainted. Cool light from an
> uncovered window out of frame.
